Arcana 1.10.6

Released October 2026. Your launcher updates you when you reconnect.

Big wins get a screen

Hit it big at the casino and the table hands you the moment:

  • BIG WIN for a round that pays ten times your stake or more, in silver — a quick flash, gone in about two seconds.
  • MEGA WIN from fifty times, in gold, with more coins and a shake.
  • MAX WIN when a game pays its most: three Gilded Runes, a Crash ridden to the ceiling, ten of ten at Keno, a run the table can't pay any more on. The screen goes dark, the title slams in, the multiplier and your chips count up, and the coins rain. Click to carry on, or it clears itself after a few seconds.

Every game shows it, from Crash and Slots to the dice and every baccarat tie. On a shared table it always clears before the next round's bets open, so it never costs you a bet.

Three Gilded Runes.

Warriors: full swings hit harder

Since 1.10.3 an early swing lands far less. Now the wait pays a Warrior back: his full swing deals 40% more, and so does his sweep. Swing early and the bonus shrinks with the charge, to next to nothing on a spam click. It counts against players too.

Smaller changes

  • A tree standing just outside a working site's fence, where the site's ground kept anyone from cutting it, blocked Fallow's crop-field gate. It's gone, and a site placed from now on clears its own edge.

Arcana 1.10.5

Released October 2026. Your launcher updates you when you reconnect.

A playtest mission: The Tower

The first chapter of a new kind of story — played as a crew, start to finish, in one sitting. Find Signaller Tamsin Rook on Asteryn's Copper Square and ask about the tower. Party members nearby come with you; anyone further off gets a link in chat to join.

  • Climb the signal tower on the ridge, relight its beacon, and hold the top until the signal goes out. Someone is listening when it does.
  • If the whole crew goes down, it's Mission failed: you're back at the last checkpoint at once, and a death inside the mission costs you nothing.
  • Everyone who finishes is paid in coin and experience and takes home Tamsin's Signal Horn.
  • A cutscene skips only when everyone watching it asks to; the screen shows who has.
  • It's a playtest: open to every level, replayable, and it doesn't touch your story progress. Tell us what you think.

Mini bosses

Five named creatures now hold a lair each, one to a country: Old Blindhand in the Ironback Deeps, Mother Harrow on the High Meadow, the Bellwrack of the Sunken Holt, the Hoarwarden on the Snowline and the Scrivener at the edge of the Grey Fold. Each has a move of its own, warned before it lands — walk out of it. Their name is on the bar. Everyone who fought one is paid a purse of coin and its country's rare materials, on top of the usual kill. One that falls stands again 30 minutes later.

Mother Harrow on the High Meadow.

Boss fights: a job for archers and mages

In their second phase the story's bosses call a Herald onto the top of their arena wall. While it stands, a tether mends the boss. Nobody can climb up to it, and it shrugs off most of a sword's blow: shoot it down, or burn it with a spell. In the last phase a swarm of small things rises and drifts to the boss to mend it, and the boss's guard holds while any of them stands — a Mage's area spell clears the pack.

A Herald on Hallowhorn's wall, tethered to the boss.

Frost freezes

Frost now locks what it hits in ice for 3 seconds: it can't move, turn or fight. The next Mage spell on it deals two and a half times its damage and shatters the ice. Bosses are never frozen but take the same bonus; a player hit by Frost is slowed for 3 seconds and marked. After a cast that catches anything, Frost waits 5 seconds; into water or lava, still 1.5. The Shatter passive is retired — if you had points in it, you get them back when you log in.

Iron and leather for every town

The island never ran short of iron: the workshops were turning all of it into goods nobody ordered. Workshops now make to what the towns ask for. The mine digs twice the iron it did, and the pens give a hide every second bale, so smiths have stock for the forge again.

Smaller changes

  • Working sites use the mod's own props — steel pipes, oil drums, crane hooks, a two-by-two millstone, saw benches — and the mill, the still house and the mine are rebuilt. The metal props can be crafted from steel.
  • The practice dummy has a head now: an arrow in its hay bale is a headshot, and the panel lists headshots on their own line.
  • Heavy dead have twice a normal body's health, not three times.
  • Early swings — before your attack bar is full — do much less, on a steep curve, so a full swing is always worth waiting for.
  • The HUD shows a spell's real cooldown when it changes.
  • Shops fit every screen size, and a keeper who only sells services no longer shows an empty basket. The Bank, Slots and Dice screens' headers fit.
  • Cutscenes: the cameras no longer clip into walls or trees, and every scene is re-shot for the ground it plays on.
  • A slow server no longer kicks you for sending your map.

Blackjack is a shared table

  • Up to five players sit at one table against one dealer. Click the croupier or the table to take the first open seat; a full table lets you watch.
  • Bets are open for 8 seconds from the first bet, or until everyone seated has bet, so a player alone is dealt at once, as before.

Arcana 1.10 — Towns That Work

Released October 2026. Your launcher updates you when you reconnect.

Every loaf, every log and every iron ingot on a counter now comes from somewhere you can walk to.

Until now a town's shelves refilled by themselves overnight. From 1.10 they do not. Each town digs, cuts, grows and makes its own goods, carries them between towns by lorry and ship, and sells you only what it actually has. A town that runs short charges more, pays more, and in the end closes the services that depend on what it lacks, until a delivery lands. You can help: sell it what it needs, carry its crates, and keep the roads and the sea open.

How it all fits together

Every good on a counter has a journey, and everything that happens along the way shows up in the price you pay and in what a town can do for you.

  1. It is gathered. A town's working sites dig, cut, grow and fish raw goods into its store. Players can sell raw goods into the store too.
  2. It is made. The workshops inside the walls turn raw goods into what the counters sell. A workshop with nothing to work with stands idle, so a town short of coal soon runs short of ingots too.
  3. It is carried. What a town does not make comes by lorry along the roads, or by ship from port to port, and every truck and ship burns Nought's diesel. A storm that sinks a ship, the dead taking a lorry on a dark stretch of road, or a town out of diesel means the delivery comes late, comes smaller, or does not come at all.
  4. It is priced. Each counter watches its own shelf. A good running low or selling fast costs more, and the counter pays you more for it too; a full shelf costs less. So a shortage in one town is a chance to earn: bring it what it lacks and it pays you well for it.
  5. It keeps the town running. Services depend on goods. A town with none of something for a whole day closes every service that needs it — no iron, no forge; no bread, no inn — until a delivery lands.

So the island is one chain. The ports keep the sea legs moving, the roads keep the lorries moving, and Nought's oil keeps both fuelled. Break a link and prices climb, then doors close, one town after another. Carrying crates, salvaging wrecks and selling towns what they need keeps every link whole.

Towns that work

Every town has working sites outside its walls. They are fenced places with their own workers, who really dig, cut, pick, shear and tend the blocks inside the fence, and what they gather goes straight into the town's store. The blocks grow back or refill, and the workers carry on while you are away: an empty site keeps counting its work and the blocks catch up when you come back.

  • Go and look. Every site is a short walk from its town's gate. Click a worker to hear what the site is doing: how much is ready to work, or that the day's work is done.

Every town has workshops inside its walls, on what used to be empty paving: Fallow's mill, bakehouse, sawmill, tannery and fletchery; Greave's forge, charcoal kilns and masons' yard; Nought's gutting house, lamp works, ropewalk, sail loft, still house and whetstone shop; Asteryn's alembic house, bindery, fitting shop, glassworks and masons' yard.   • Each workshop turns the store's raw goods into what the counters sell: wheat into bread, logs into planks and sticks, iron ore and coal into ingots, crude oil into diesel.
  • Its two workers are only busy when there is something to work with. If a workshop's hands stand idle, click one and they tell you what the store is short of, for example "Idle: the store is short of Coal, and nothing is made until it comes in."

Goods that travel

A town only makes some things. Everything else is carried to it from another town, as a real lorry or ship that you can meet on the road or at sea. When no player is near, the delivery still runs on its schedule.

  • Lorries replace the wagons on the four roads to the Crossmark Halt, and carry goods from the harbours to Greave, Fallow and Asteryn.
  • Fuel tankers carry diesel from the harbours to Greave and Asteryn.
  • Livestock trucks run beside the lorries on the roads to the Halt, carrying animals from Fallow's pen to the other towns.
  • Ships sail between the ports, one on each sea leg. Motor cargo ships — steel hulls, a bridge aft, a smoking funnel and a bow wave — run the main legs; oil tankers sail beside them on the two diesel legs, from Nought to Asteryn Harbour and from Porthold to the West Harbour; and the sailing ship keeps the short crossing from Nought to Porthold. Nought's old lighter is retired.

Trucks and ships burn diesel. Every departure burns diesel from its home town's store: 8 for a lorry or a tanker, 6 for a livestock truck, 32 for the sailing ship, 48 for a motor cargo ship and 64 for an oil tanker. Diesel is made only at Nought's still, from the crude oil of Nought's well. If a town runs out of diesel, its tankers, livestock trucks and ships stay home until a delivery brings more — and its lorries go out as donkey wagons instead: a big wagon pulled by a donkey, slower than the old ox wagon and carrying half what a lorry does.

Driving a crate pays more. Hauling a crate for the carriers' post works as before. If you carry it in a vehicle, you get a bigger crate for the same fee: twice the size in a cart, three times in a traction engine, four times in a lorry.

Storms at sea

About one day in sixty is a storm day. You will know it: the sky turns to thunder. A ship at sea on a storm day may be wrecked, and a long crossing is in more danger than a short one.

  • A wreck washes up on the shore nearest to where the ship sank. The town crier tells everyone which ship went down and where its wreck lies: how far and in which direction from the nearest place, and its coordinates.
  • Salvage it. Every sealed crate you pull out of a wreck pays you the carrier's fee at any carriers' yard or at the Crossmark Halt, and its goods go on to the town that was waiting for them.
  • Be quick. A wreck lasts three days. After that, the weather takes what is left.

Send your mount by sea or by truck

You can now send your own horse, donkey, mule, camel or llama to another town.

  1. Ride it up to a stable hand. There is one at every port, at every town's carriers' yard and at the Crossmark Halt.
  2. Click her. She lists every place a ship or a livestock truck from here can take your mount. Press [Send]. It costs 10 coin.
  3. Collect it. When it arrives, go to the stable hand at that end and press [Collect]. Your mount comes back exactly as it left, with its saddle, its chest and its name.
  • Click any stable hand to see where your mounts are: waiting to leave, on the way, or stabled at the far end.
  • If the ship is wrecked with your mount aboard, you are told. Salvage the wreck before it is gone and your mount goes on to where it was going. If nobody salvages it in time, your mount is lost.

Prices that move

Prices now follow each town's shelves. A good that is running low or selling fast costs more. A good that fills its shelf or that players keep selling to the town costs less. Each counter moves on its own, and the change wears off by half every real-world day, offline hours included.

  • A price never falls below half its usual level and never rises above 1.6 times it.
  • The counter tells you why. When a price has moved by 5% or more, its card says so, for example "▲ 12% — the shelf is low". The four reasons are: the shelf is low, the shelf is full, it has been selling fast, and people have been selling a lot of it here.
  • What a counter pays you for goods moves the same way, and the "Wanted here" list shows ▲ or ▼ beside it.

Only the goods a town makes or carries move. Services, lessons, deeds and the like keep their prices.

Fittings

Steel, Nought and silicon fittings are now made in town workshops, so the counters that sell them can run short. You can also craft them yourself: the recipes are in the Engineer's Guide. Crafting a fitting needs the same lesson as buying one: if you have not been taught it, the crafting grid stays empty and tells you who teaches it and where. The brass fitting is still open to everyone, as before.

The dead: tougher, and fewer

The dead were still far too many. From 1.10 each one is much harder to kill, and you will very rarely see more than five at once.

  • Tougher. Each of the dead has 3 times the health, 5 times the Armor and 3 times the Magic Resist it had. At level 1 a body is about three times as hard to kill; at level 20 about five times.
  • Five at most. Outside a blood moon, no horde grows past five, and no more than five of the dead come for one player at a time. A noise or a moan draws the nearest few and no more.
  • Blood moons are still big, but a little smaller than before: the crowd near you is twice an ordinary night's (4 to 10) instead of three times. Watchtower raids shrink with it.
  • Fights placed by the story and the lairs have a quarter of the bodies they had, so the effort stays about the same. The heavies stay: every wave that had a heavy still has one, with at least one more beside it. A lair's pack is now 2 or 3 of the dead and its Warden.
  • They pay more. A kill of the dead pays 1.75 times the experience it did, and twice the coin (4 coin instead of 2).

Also changed

  • A ship's storm wreck is named where it lies, on the shore, so you never have to search open water for it.
  • Outposts' sites pay their owner town.

Not yet

  • Defending the routes against the dead is not a job yet.
  • Livestock and crude oil cannot be sold by players.

1.10 in short

  • Towns that work — every town has fenced working sites outside its walls and workshops inside them, with workers who really dig, cut, grow and make what the counters sell. Shelves no longer refill by themselves.
  • Goods that travel — lorries, fuel tankers, livestock trucks, motor cargo ships, oil tankers and a sailing ship carry goods between towns. They burn Nought's diesel; a town without any sends its lorries out as donkey wagons.
  • Seven ports — four container terminals with gantry cranes and three outposts that work for their towns, all joined to their towns by new roads.
  • Storms — about one day in sixty, a ship may be wrecked. Its wreck washes ashore, the crier says where, and you have three days to salvage it for pay.
  • Send your mount — 10 coin at any stable hand to send your horse by ship or truck.
  • Prices move — scarce goods cost more and plentiful ones less, and the counter tells you why.
  • Sell raw goods to towns — for 90% of what they charge, paid from the town's treasury.
  • Shortages close services — a day without iron closes the forge, a day without bread closes the inn.
  • Every town needs the others — keep the roads and the sea open and every town stays open.
  • The dead — three to five times tougher, five at most outside a blood moon, a quarter as many in placed fights, and better pay.

Arcana 1.9.4

Released October 2026. Your launcher updates you when you reconnect.

Armour, reworked

  • Every tier steps up plainly. Armour toughness is gone, and its worth is folded into the armour number itself, so hide, iron, steel, diamond and netherite now climb in clear steps and each tier sits above every plate of the tier below. A diamond cuirass reads +56 against iron's +32. No piece protects less than it did before.
  • Boss plates sit above the best forged netherite.
  • The Blessings of Fortitude and Ironhide give armour in place of their toughness.

Greave's crew at work.

Released September 2026. Your launcher updates you when you reconnect.

Combat lag fix

Hits and actions felt delayed, even on LAN. The cause was how this Minecraft version handles player input: the server only read what you did at the start of each tick. Every hit waited up to 50 ms before the server saw it, which felt exactly like ping.

The server now handles your input the moment it arrives, so hits register as soon as you swing. We also gave the server more memory, which cuts the short stutters from its memory cleanup.

Weapons and the Forge

What each kind of weapon does

The answers to his two questions, since they are now written down:

  • Locking on. The Mage's Arcane Bolt locks on to a creature within 15° of your aim and 14 blocks of the bolt — the same on Wand, Staff and Sceptre. Bows do not lock on at all.
  • Range. A bow's reach is how fast its arrows leave it. Bare, before any parts: the Shortbow draws fully in 0.77 s and shoots at plain-bow speed, the Longbow draws in 0.95 s at 1.10×, and the Warbow draws in 1.00 s at 1.34×. The Shuriken flies 12 blocks, through everything in its path.

What slots and traits are

  • A weapon has three slots. Core and Grip fit any weapon; the third is the weapon's own — Edge for a blade, Limb for a bow, Focus for a Mage's weapon.
  • On a bow, the core's damage is draw speed, the grip's speed is arrow speed, and the limb adds arrow damage. On a Mage's weapon the focus adds spell damage.

  • Traits come from parts. Each part carries one at most — a small spark marks the ones that do. A weapon holds three at most, and the same trait twice counts once.

  • A LOOSE PART'S DAMAGE AND SPEED "melee only" ⇒ "melee; draw speed on a bow" and "melee; arrow speed on a bow". It had been wrong since bows started reading those two numbers, and told Archers their best core did nothing.

Arcana Patch 1.0.3

Released 22 September 2026.

The big one is worn storage: four containers you wear like armour, none of which cost you a square of your pack. Beyond that this patch is repair — twenty-seven landmarks that the world was supposed to contain and never did, an item encyclopedia that had never once run, every boss re-tuned after the tool that priced them turned out to be blind, and a trophy board that paid a fifth of what killing for it was worth.

Existing worlds and characters carry over. No save data is reset. The landmarks were added to the world that is already running — no character, building, chest or quest was touched.

Patch Highlights

  • Four worn containers. A Backpack, a quiver, a smith's roll and a trophy bag, each in its own inventory slot, none of them taking pack space. B opens the Backpack.
  • The item encyclopedia (JEI) had never run at all. Every recipe, component, pattern and where to buy it card in this mod was dead. All of it works now.
  • Every boss had about a third of the health it needed. The bench that priced them could not see the damage half of the game.
  • A trophy paid about a fifth of what the kills to fill it were worth. Reported by a player, and understated.
  • Twenty-seven landmarks were missing from the world. 1.0.2's notes said three.
  • Arrows in a worn quiver are used by your bow. They were not, and the bow would not even draw.

Storage

Four things you wear, and none of them costs you a slot

Each has its own square in the inventory screen, beside the armour. Left-click takes one off, exactly like a helmet. Nothing gates anything: you can own the quiver without the Backpack.

  • KEYBINDNone→B opens your Backpack
  • COMMANDSNone→/worn backpack, /worn quiver, /worn smiths_roll, /worn trophy_bag

The Backpack is the cheapest counter in the game's own line, and deliberately has no level gate. The wide Backpack is a separate item rather than an upgrade to the one you wear, so the old one is yours to sell on.

Eighteen slots is the ceiling and it is deliberate. Past two rows the specialised containers stop mattering and the pressure the whole system exists to ease simply dissolves.

Nothing nests — no container goes inside any container, including the Backpack, which otherwise holds anything. Everything in them survives death, like the rest of your inventory.

The Item Encyclopedia

Just Enough Items — the tab that tells you what a thing is, what it is made of and where to buy it.

  • EVERYTHING THIS MOD ADDS TO ITNothing→72 components, 17 patterns, 18 attunements, 12 shops

The whole integration had never once run. Nine files and four recipe categories, none of them ever invoked: a player asking where to find scrivener's ink could not find the components at all, and the answer was neither the fog nor his character — the plugin was never loaded. One line of configuration.

What hid it was a log line from a different mechanism printing "jei is loaded" on every launch. The log said the feature was alive while the feature had never run.

Card colours — the shop's own name on a Where to buy card was drawn in gold on a light grey panel, which measures 1.12:1 against it. That is not low contrast, it is none.

  • TEXT ON A CARDMinecraft's HUD colours, five of them unreadable→a palette that clears 4.5:1

The Trophy Board

A player, unprompted: "The monster part balancing is weird. They sell for like 4 copper. I can kill 2 zombies and get more gold than killing a named monster part."

He was right, and understated it. The board's own rule is that a lot pays between a quarter and a half again the coin the kills that filled it already paid. All 23 rows were outside it.

  • HOUND HACKLE 16 kills worth 128 coin, lot paid 23 — 0.18×
  • BENT STRUT 9 kills worth 72, lot paid 19 — 0.26×

  • BRINE BEZOAR 9 kills worth 72, lot paid 35 — 0.49×

Killing pays slightly less and the board pays properly, so hunting a named part is worth the walk. An hour at level 20 goes from about 3420 coin to about 2700 from kills alone, with the board making up the difference and more.

Every blessing fee was re-derived from the new income so the same amount of play still buys a rung.

Arcana Patch 1.0.2

Released 20 September 2026.

This patch reworks the Assassin, adds the first act of an endgame questline, adds a Training Grounds practice mode, and gives every character health as they level. It also corrects a crafting recipe that could not be made, a quest step nobody could follow, and four objectives that pointed at the wrong person.

Existing worlds and characters carry over. No save data is reset by this patch. Skill points already spent stay spent, and the new health is applied to your existing character the next time you log in.

All players need this client update. Quest text, the new items and the Training Grounds button are client-side.

Patch Highlights

  • The Assassin is reworked: Wound, a new stacking effect, plus shurikens and three daggers that now do different jobs.
  • Characters gain health as they level. Previously they gained almost none.
  • An endgame questline begins — its first chapter, for characters from level 8.
  • Shears could not be crafted. A component recipe was using the same shape.

Classes

Assassin

The Assassin had no unconditional damage of any kind. Her entire offensive kit was a backstab bonus, which returns nothing unless she is behind the target — and creatures in this game turn to face you. Head-on she was swinging raw weapon damage with a health penalty and no bonus at all.

Melee Damage

  • ALL MELEE HITS+0%→+20%

This applies whether or not she is behind the target.

Backstab Arc

  • ARC COUNTED AS BEHIND120 degree rear cone→180 degrees, anything past the target's shoulder

The backstab multiplier is unchanged at 1.75, or 2.15 with The Deeper Cut.

A Kill Restores Full Health (New)

  • ON LANDING A KILLING BLOWNo effect→Health restored to maximum

Class-wide and unconditional. No skill point buys it. It is a reward for finishing a fight and not a way out of one going badly: a bad opening still kills you, and a good one leaves you whole for the next fight. A creature that bleeds to death from Wound counts as your kill.

Wound (New)

Your melee hits stack a Wound on a creature. Each creature keeps its own count.

  • STACKSN/A→Up to 3, one per melee hit
  • ARMOUR IGNORED0%→25% per stack (75% at three)
  • DAMAGE+0%→+15% per stack (+45% at three)
  • BLEEDNone→2 damage per second per stack
  • AT THREE STACKSN/A→+25% damage to that creature while you stay on it
  • DECAYN/A→5 seconds after your last hit on that creature
  • AGAINST PLAYERSN/A→Does not apply

Leaving a creature does not carry its Wound to the next one, which starts at zero.

Only five creatures in the game have any armour at all — the Boltcairn (12), Slabback (10), Stonelung (8), Hollowbole (6) and Longhand (3). Every other creature has none. Armour shred alone would therefore have done nothing in most fights, which is why Wound raises damage as well as shredding.

  • STILETTO2.9→2.2 attacks per second. Damage 0.0 ⇒ 0.6. Durability 35 ⇒ 45
  • RIPPER Unchanged

Shurikens (New)

Held in the offhand and thrown with right-click while a dagger stays in your main hand. A shuriken flies flat and passes through every creature in its path, damaging each one, then disappears.

  • RANGEN/A→12 blocks, then gone. It does not arc, land, or drop
  • DAMAGEN/A→6 forged, 3.5 crude, to every creature it passes through
  • COOLDOWNN/A→1.5 seconds between throws

  • IN COMBAT+15%→+15%, plus a further 10% while a fight is live

Progression

Health Per Level

A health curve existed but was scaled to a level cap of 50, which this game does not have. A character had therefore gained 14% of the intended amount by the end of the storyline and 39% at the cap. A level 8 Assassin had 20.6 health against a level 1's 18.0.

  • HEALTH PER LEVEL+0.6% of base→+7.89% of base
  • AT LEVEL 8+5%→+55%
  • AT LEVEL 20+39%→+150%

This is a percentage, so the differences between classes are preserved at every level. It applies to existing characters on your next login.

This is not the whole fix. Every class still dies faster at level 8 than at level 1 against the same creature, because new characters receive a large damage reduction that is removed at level 5. See Known Issues.

Combat Balance

Every storyline fight has been rebalanced and is now measured for all four classes against the creature its own step sends them at.

The early-game damage grace

New characters took 65% less damage until level 5, and health grows about 8% a level — so the game withdrew a 2.86× protection against a 32% gain, and you died faster at level 8 than at level 1.

  • DAMAGE TAKEN AT LEVEL 135%→60%
  • SECONDS TO DIE, LEVEL 110.3→6.7
  • SECONDS TO DIE, LEVEL 54.7→5.3
  • SECONDS TO DIE, LEVEL 209.0→10.0

Every level now sits above the five-second survival floor, and the swing across the early game is 1.26× where it was 2.86×.

Arcana Patch 1.0.1

Released 19 September 2026.

This patch addresses issues found during the first multiplayer playtest. It covers class balance, night visibility, town protection, two interface screens that could not display all of their content, and a rewrite of every ability, blessing and passive description.

Existing worlds and characters carry over. No save data is reset by this patch.

Patch Highlights

  • The Archer's Hawk Eye passive works again, and headshots now deal additional damage.
  • The Mage's basic attack costs mana and has a longer cooldown, with its damage raised to match.
  • Night is significantly brighter than 1.0.0, and can now be adjusted in game.
  • Towns can no longer be dug into. Fallow's protected area was too small and has been corrected.
  • The Path screen can be dragged. Parts of it were previously unreachable at common resolutions.
